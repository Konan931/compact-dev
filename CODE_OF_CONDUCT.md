# Code of Conduct

`compact-dev` is a technical project built around clarity, auditability, and constructive collaboration. Participation is welcome when it helps keep the project useful, safe, and understandable.

## Expected behavior

Contributors, maintainers, reviewers, and users are expected to:

- communicate respectfully, including during strong technical disagreement;
- critique code, design, evidence, and decisions rather than people;
- give enough context for others to reproduce, review, or challenge a claim;
- accept corrections and update conclusions when better evidence appears;
- respect privacy, attribution, boundaries, and reasonable requests to disengage;
- keep reviews focused on the contribution and the documented project scope.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following is not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or sustained personal attacks;
- discriminatory or demeaning language directed at a person or group;
- sexualized attention or other unwanted personal conduct;
- publishing private information, credentials, personal data, or identifying details without permission;
- deliberately disrupting issues, reviews, discussions, or project infrastructure;
- using security reports, bug reports, or review processes to pressure, expose, or target people.

## Technical disagreement

Disagreement is normal and often useful. Be precise about assumptions, evidence, tradeoffs, and failure modes. Maintainers may close repetitive, off-topic, non-actionable, or abusive threads without treating disagreement itself as misconduct.

## Scope

This code applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, project-managed communication, and other spaces where someone is representing or participating in `compact-dev`.

## Reporting and enforcement

Do not publish sensitive conduct reports, personal information, or security vulnerabilities in a public issue.

For conduct concerns, use an appropriate private GitHub reporting or contact mechanism available to you. Platform-level abuse can also be reported directly to GitHub. Maintainers should handle reports as privately as practical, avoid conflicts of interest where possible, and consider the available evidence before acting.

Depending on severity and repetition, responses may include a private correction, warning, temporary participation restriction, removal of content, or permanent exclusion from project-managed spaces.

Retaliation against someone for raising a good-faith concern is itself unacceptable.
